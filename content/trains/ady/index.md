The Baku – Tbilisi trains belong to Azerbaijan Railways (ADY). Two completely different trains run on the route:

- **No. 37/38** — modern sleeping cars built by the Swiss company Stadler, with Comfort, Comfort+ and Lux classes;
- **No. 39/40** — refurbished Soviet-type cars, with Standard (open-plan sleeper, *platzkart*), Standard+ (4-berth compartment, *kupe*) and Business (2-berth sleeper, *SV*) classes.

In summer, extra Soviet-type cars were attached to the Stadler train, and such changes may happen again. Either way, when buying a ticket, go by the class name: it tells you the type of car.

## Train 37/38 (Stadler)

The cars were built in 2019–2020 but only entered service on the route in May 2026. The train has seven sleeping cars and a dining car.

Compartments open with an electronic key card, and the same card opens your personal safe. Every berth has its own light and power socket. The compartment has a control panel for the lights, temperature and announcement volume, and a button to call the attendant. There is Wi-Fi. On the screen in the compartment you can watch films, listen to music and follow the train on a map; you can also connect to the system from your phone. The attendant gives out headphones for free. All classes get bed linen, a travel kit and a hand towel.

The dining car has 28 seats; food is paid for separately.

### Comfort

A 4-berth compartment. Luggage goes under the lower berths and on the shelf above. The shower and toilet are shared by the car. A bath kit costs extra.

{{< train-gallery "comfort" >}}

### Comfort+

A 2-berth compartment with an extra seat and a washbasin. The shower and toilet are shared by the car. A bath kit costs extra.

Comfort+ and Lux compartments are in the same car and separated by a sliding wall. When it is opened, you get a family compartment for four.

{{< train-gallery "comfort-plus" >}}

### Lux

A 2-berth compartment with an extra seat and its own shower and toilet. Besides the hand towel, you get a bath towel. Lux passengers can wait for the train in the VIP lounges at the stations, which have water, tea, coffee, sweets and Wi-Fi.

## Train 39/40 (Soviet-type cars)

Soviet-type cars after refurbishment. All classes get bed linen, a towel and a travel kit. Food is sold from carts going through the cars, and you can also order it to your seat via a QR code. There are no showers on the train.

### Standard (platzkart)

An open-plan car with bays of four berths: two lower and two upper. Side berths are only in the front part of the car: the upper one folds up, and the lower one turns into a table. There are no ladders; you climb to the upper berths using metal supports. Instead of side rails, the upper berths have two safety straps each. Every berth has a curtain and a lamp, but the lights are switched on and off for the whole car at once. One power socket per bay. Toilets are at both ends of the car.

{{< train-gallery "standard" >}}

### Standard+ (kupe)

A 4-berth compartment. Luggage goes under the lower berths and on the metal shelves above. Every berth has a reading lamp at the head, and there is one power socket for the whole compartment.

{{< train-gallery "standard-plus" >}}

### Business (SV)

A 2-berth compartment, luggage goes under the berths. Each passenger has their own power socket and lamp. There is a folding table, a TV and a button to call the attendant. Toilets are at both ends of the car.
